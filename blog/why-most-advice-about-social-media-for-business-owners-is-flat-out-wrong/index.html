Why Most Advice About Social Media for Business Owners Is Flat-Out Wrong

August 12, 2026

Someone with 200,000 followers told you to post three times a day. A consultant said you need to go all-in on short-form video. A peer swore that LinkedIn is dead and Threads is the future. You tried all of it. Nothing stuck.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: most social media advice is wrong — not because the people giving it are dishonest, but because advice almost always flows from personal experience that doesn't transfer. As Anne Helen Petersen put it, advice is bad because it "prescriptively flows from personal experience" — experiences that happened in different markets, different seasons, and to different people with different resources.

Advice that worked for a full-time creator in 2019 is not a strategy for a business owner running operations in 2026.

The Generalization Trap

Most social media frameworks were built by people whose entire job is social media. They have editors, schedulers, videographers, and analytics teams. When they say "just be consistent," they are not accounting for the business owner who also runs payroll, manages clients, and takes sales calls before 9am.

Consistency is real advice. But without context, it becomes a guilt loop — you know you should post, you don't have time, you feel behind, you post something rushed, it underperforms, and you conclude that social media doesn't work for your business.

It works. The approach just wasn't built for you.

What Actually Transfers

Here is a framework worth keeping: before you take any social media advice, ask three questions.

First — does this person have my constraints? Time, team size, and budget matter enormously. Second — is this advice channel-specific or genuinely multi-channel? A tactic that works on TikTok often fails on LinkedIn, and vice versa. Third — when did this work for them? The half-life of social media tactics is short. What dominated in 2023 may be algorithmic noise today.

If the advice doesn't survive those three questions, set it down.

The Automation Misunderstanding

One of the most misrepresented topics in the advice ecosystem is automation. You have probably heard that scheduled posts kill reach, that AI content feels hollow, or that audiences can tell when a human isn't behind every word.

Some of that was true — for early, clunky tools that stripped context and blasted identical content across every platform. It is not true for intelligent automation that adapts tone, formats content natively per channel, and maintains the brand voice you have spent years building.

The business owners winning on social media right now are not posting more. They are posting smarter — with systems that handle the execution while they focus on the thinking.
